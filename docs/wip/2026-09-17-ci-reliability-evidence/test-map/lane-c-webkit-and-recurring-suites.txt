PART 1b — THE "STORM", AND WHETHER THE ISOLATION CLAIM CAN BE PROVED DETERMINISTICALLY
================================================================================

WHAT THE STORM IS HOPING TO PROVOKE
-----------------------------------
Mechanically the storm is two `await paneOne.handleWorktreeProductInvalidation(.filesChanged(...))` calls
with batchSeq 702 and 703, on the same path "tracked.txt", back to back on MainActor
(BridgeProductWebKitTwoPaneJourneyTestSupport.swift:353-370). There is a third and fourth invalidation
earlier in the journey (701 at :448-456, 704 at :472-481).

Two events, same path, same lane, sequential `await`s on one actor is not a storm in any concurrency sense
— nothing races. What the sequence is actually hoping for is a *scheduling* outcome: that an invalidation
arriving while the pane is `.loadedHidden` is merged into the dirty fact and NOT allowed to claim a
refresh reservation. The relevant production code is `dirtyFactByLane` merge at BridgePaneRefreshAdmission-
Coordinator.swift:306/:323 and :562-577, the acquisition guard `guard activity == .foreground` at :753,
and the pass counter at :535. The second invalidation exists to prove merging (two events, one dirty
fact, zero passes) rather than to provoke a race.

So the honest reading: the storm is not probing an interleaving at all. It is asserting a scheduling
policy — and three of its four oracles (refreshPassCount frozen at :157, dirtyGeneration present at :156,
provider comparisonCount frozen at support :375-380) measure exactly that policy correctly. Only the
fourth (the metadata sequence at :158-162) reaches for a transport counter that the policy does not own.

    is no gap.

    every failure carries `pane=+0[], file=+0[], review=+0[]`
    (BridgeProductWebKitMetadataStormDiagnostic.swift:12-20, :30-32) — the product-work counters, at zero,

    Changing which number the `#expect` reads, using values the proof already computes
    (TwoPaneJourneyTestSupport:349, :374), would have fixed this without touching production code.

    in a lane (`mise run test:architecture`, .mise.toml:343-349) that the aggregate already runs
    (.mise.toml:322). The most expensive test in the six is also the only outright duplicate.
